# Deployment and client exit arrangements

Recorded 25 October 2026 for checklist task 10-7. Boniface maintains one configurable platform with web, mobile, admin and API applications. This is the proposed operating arrangement and handover specification under his best-judgment build approval. It is not an executed client contract, production installation, export feature, restore result or acceptance of a release gate. Realtime operator and account-control decisions remain open.

The standard planning allowances are five staff, up to 300 active listings, 10 GB of retained media and up to two hours of routine support monthly. They are proposed commercial boundaries, not accepted contract terms, enforced quotas or an incident-response service level. Export work, dedicated infrastructure and bespoke changes are not silently unlimited within these allowances. Record any agreed setup or exit assistance and its price before work that incurs external cost. No setup fee is currently agreed.

Every deployment needs an inventory of exact source/config/schema versions, verified domains and app context, database/runtime identities, media and job namespaces, backup destinations, notification sender, domain/store/signing ownership, support contact, costs and approved features. Keep secret references in that inventory; values belong in managed secret storage. Never place provider credentials in public manifests, browser props, source, exported data or this document. Rotate or revoke delegated access through the account owner's approved process when access changes.

## Ownership and authorization

Boniface controls the independently written replacement source. Client use, source access, any licence grant and dedicated hosting rights need explicit recorded terms; paying for a service is not recorded as a transfer of the reusable platform. Existing private contribution/access rules remain in [contributing.md](contributing.md). Vendor code, designs, photographs, fonts and client records retain their unresolved provenance/permission boundaries. This document grants no rights over them.

The client names an authorized data controller/contact and export recipient before real records are imported or disclosed. Record which company, record classes, date scope, third-party assets and historical private/audit material that person may receive. Staff membership alone does not authorize a bulk export. Platform support access must be expressly scoped, time bounded, attributable and auditable; broad support/export administration is not currently implemented. A dedicated client never receives another company's data or shared-service credentials.

Identity exports use stable internal IDs and permitted external-subject mappings so the receiving system can invite users or re-establish access. Do not export plaintext passwords, password hashes, bearer/refresh tokens, signing private keys, MFA recovery material or provider secrets. Account recovery and store/signing transfers need their own verified owner workflows.

## Required company export package

The following is the proposed implementation contract. No package of real client records has been produced or verified.

Use a versioned UTF-8 manifest recording company ID, export ID, exact application/schema/protocol versions, extraction instant/window, included and deliberately excluded record classes, row/object counts, file sizes and SHA-256 checksums. Record retention restrictions and unresolved/incomplete objects explicitly. Preserve stable IDs and relationships; distinguish current public approved snapshots from private drafts and historical receipts. Exact money uses integer minor units plus currency and rental period; dates use canonical UTC instants. Preserve location precision and reference identity without inventing or merging labels.

Proposed data files contain the authorized company metadata, staff/identity mappings, properties/listings/revisions, saved reference facts, publication/availability history, enquiries, contact and viewing history, and permitted operation/audit history. Use documented JSON Lines for nested/versioned records; offer CSV projections only with a field dictionary and explicit omissions. CSV summaries cannot replace immutable relational history. Include only the allowed media bytes with manifest associations, descriptions, cover/order facts and provenance/rights restrictions. Quarantined, incomplete or unlicensed originals are not silently promoted to approved public media.

An export worker must resolve the company and privileged authority on the server, enforce current authorization, bound work and record who requested/completed/downloaded it. It needs a consistent database/media extraction strategy, cancellation/failure reporting, safe resume and an expiring private delivery method without credentials or private object paths in public URLs. Those controls, any export job/receipt schema and deletion/expiry mechanism remain to be built and tested. Do not work around them by handing a client the shared database backup.

Before delivery, independently verify no foreign-company rows/objects, count and relationship reconciliation, hashes/actual bytes, exact money/time semantics, allowed private fields, completeness and the exclusions manifest. Re-import into disposable isolated storage and verify representative public and authorized staff journeys, approved pointers and media references. Test recipient authorization, expiry/revocation, failed/interrupted jobs and replay without duplicate disclosure. Preserve the source snapshot until the designated recipient confirms receipt and the agreed retention decision permits deletion. Export acceptance belongs to the remaining implementation, staging and handover tasks.

## Exit, continuity and retention

1. Verify the requester and receiving custodian, agree scope/format, permitted histories/media, destination, responsibility, costs and support window. Record any unresolved rights, active enquiry/viewing obligations or legal hold before scheduling changes.
2. Rehearse the export and import/recovery using synthetic or explicitly authorized records. Agree recovery-point and recovery-time objectives from measured results; none is promised yet. Record notification, rollback triggers, freeze/delta reconciliation and responsibility for writes created after the snapshot.
3. Produce and reconcile the authorized final package. Confirm the recipient can decrypt/access it through a separate approved secret channel and can use the documented format. Record acceptance or defects; no automatic release of all shared infrastructure access follows.
4. Change traffic, domains, application distribution or account access only within separately approved cutover/transfer scope. Keep the old live system recoverable through the agreed rollback window. A cancelled service, exported ZIP or source backup does not authorize old-system retirement.
5. Apply the agreed retention/correction/deletion decision to active records, derived media, temporary exports and independently held backups. Record retention periods, hold exceptions, backup expiry, responsible custodian and evidence in the final agreement. Existing immutable histories and synthetic storage have no complete production deletion/retention workflow; do not claim immediate erasure or remove audit/receipt history ad hoc.
6. Revoke unneeded delegated roles, runtime credentials, delivery links and notification routes after continuity checks and owner approval. Record remaining archival custody, outstanding support obligations, deletion/expiry evidence and final handover acceptance. Do not delete shared accounts/resources belonging to continuing companies.

The operational owner is Boniface; the client must designate its data/cutover approver and receiving custodian. Provider account owners perform sensitive recovery or credential transfers through their own reviewed account controls. Until those people, production retention policy, tested export/restore and approved release are recorded, the old service remains available and Stage 10/12–14 gates remain open.
